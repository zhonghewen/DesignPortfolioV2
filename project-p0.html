The Problem

Low-signal inputs were breaking the AI pipeline.

Amazon Sellers often face complex issues. When they seek support, they frequently provide vague or incomplete information, resulting in input quality too low for automated systems to resolve.

The gap between what sellers naturally provide and what AI models actually need creates a bottleneck across the entire support funnel.

Low-Signal (What we received)
"human" "111" "FBA"

Sellers typing vague keywords into open text fields. No actionable intent for an AI model to process — this data is noise.

High-Signal (What the model needs)
Intent Update product title
ASIN B001234567
New title "Laptop Backpack, for 16" laptop, lightweight, black"

A complete description with identifiers, documentation, and desired outcome — everything the model needs for confident automated resolution.

The Goal: Redesign the support funnel end-to-end — guiding sellers to provide the right signal, confirming their intent, and making automated execution feel safe enough to trust — without adding friction at any step.

02 / 09

The Opportunity

Treating the support journey as a conversion funnel.

I analyzed the end-to-end support journey as a conversion funnel. The goal was to maximize automated resolution by improving data quality at two critical stages —

  1. The Intake Stage
  2. The Clarification Stage

And

  1. Increasing seller adoption of automated execution.

The Strategy

9 experiments. 3 defining pillars.

While I led 9 distinct design experiments across the support ecosystem last year, the following three represent the core strategy — each targeting a different point of signal loss in the funnel.

Experiment A

Attach Issues

Evidence-Based Intake — shifting from descriptive text to objective evidence attached directly to the query.

Experiment B

Rephrase Issues

Guided Structural Refinement — replacing blank text fields with a structured "mad-libs" flow that fills model data requirements slot by slot.

Experiment C

Automated Execution

End-to-End Resolution — moving from instructional support to a system that performs the fix on behalf of the seller, with transparent status feedback to build trust.

04 / 09

Experiment A

Evidence-Based Intake — from "write" to "share."

The Problem: The Burden of Precision

Sellers struggled to articulate technical problems in writing. Vague, "low-signal" descriptions failed to trigger backend models — leading to frustration and immediate human escalation.

Root Cause Investigation

  • 3-Year Research Synthesis: Identified psychological barriers including fear of inaccuracy and identification anxiety. A dominant "Minimum Effort" behavior emerged — busy sellers avoided complex typing.
  • 1,000-Entry Production Audit: Revealed that critical signals were already present in artifacts (emails, logs, reports) sellers possessed — but they lacked a low-friction way to share them.

The Hypothesis: Transitioning to an evidence-first model would bypass typing friction and anxiety by capturing "ground truth" data directly from existing artifacts.

"I have the email right here from the carrier, but I don't know which part of it you need to see. I'm afraid if I type the wrong ID, my case will just be closed."

— Amazon Seller

"I just want it fixed. I don't have time to type out a 5-paragraph essay describing an error code you guys already have in your system. Can't I just show you a picture?"

— Amazon Seller

The Solution: From "Write" to "Share"

  • Rich-Format Attachments: Integrated a seamless way to upload screenshots of errors or link specific system records (Shipment IDs, ASINs) early in the flow.
  • Factual Grounding: By shifting focus from descriptive text to objective evidence, we gave AI models the "ground truth" needed for high-confidence classification.
The Strategic Move

Breaking the Deadlock

The Resistance: Engineering initially rejected "Attachment," citing high infrastructure costs and immature AI image-analysis tools (early 2024).

The Action: I used internal tools to run a "smoke test" — demonstrating that existing L1 models could already parse metadata and autofill description fields from attachments.

The Result: This proof of existing capability turned a rejected concept into a Tier-1 roadmap item.

Experiment B

Guided Structural Refinement — from anxiety to confidence.

The Problem: The Verification Gap

Even when sellers provided evidence, they feared the system might misinterpret their intent. Without definitive confirmation of the problem type, the model's resolution confidence remained low — and the lack of structured data created significant operational friction, causing unnecessary human escalations.

The UX Challenge: Defining "Good" Data

  • Synthesizing Needs: I bridged the gap between Applied Science partners (who needed specific "slots" filled like IDs and error codes) and sellers (who just wanted a fix).
  • Strategic Iteration: I defined the "Target Description" and iterated based on what was technically feasible for the model and behaviorally realistic for a stressed seller.

The Solution: Guided Structural Scaffolding

The "Rephrase & Prompt" interaction replaced the anxiety of writing from scratch with a guided confidence-building process — transforming a blank text field into a "mad-libs" structure where sellers only fill in the high-signal gaps the model actually needs.

Before — open text field
Describe your issue...
↓   Intent detected: "Update product listing"
After — structured prompt
Update title for B001234567 to "Laptop Backpack, for 16" laptop, lightweight, black"
The Designer's Edge

High-Fidelity Logic

I didn't just design the UI — I designed the data mapping. Working with Scientists, I ensured every UI "blank" mapped directly to a required model "slot," turning the interface into a visual representation of the AI's data needs.

Outcome: Eliminated "Missing Data" as a reason for failed automated resolutions.

06 / 09

Experiment C

Automated Execution — designing trust through transparency.

The Problem: Execution Error & Latency Anxiety

Users often chose the wrong path during manual execution. Fully automating the fix introduced a "processing gap" that made users anxious — they didn't know if the system was working or had stalled.

The Solution: Managing Latency to Build Trust

We moved from "Instructional" support to "Automated Execution" — where the system performs the fix on behalf of the seller:

  • Verification Loop: Before execution, a "Confirmation Card" summarized the exact change about to occur — eliminating errors from misunderstood intent.
  • Informative Status Transitions: Standard loaders were replaced with granular status updates at each processing step, so sellers always knew what the system was doing.
Change confirmed Seller reviewed and approved the exact update
Verifying shipment records... Checking eligibility and system constraints
Applying update Writing change to backend systems
Complete Confirmation sent to seller
The Strategic Learning

Designing for Trust

Users weren't impatient with the time — they were impatient with the silence. By designing transparent status updates, we increased user tolerance for system latency by over 40%, giving the model the time it needed to safely execute complex changes.

Outcome: Significant reduction in human re-contacts for the same issue.

Reflections

What I learned designing at the human-AI boundary.

  • 01. Designing for the "Lazy" User The biggest challenge wasn't the AI's capability — it was human motivation. The highest-leverage design decisions reduced effort to near zero, meeting sellers exactly where their behavior already was rather than asking them to change.
  • 02. Data as a Design Material Treating "model confidence scores" as a primary design constraint transformed how I approached interaction design. The interface became a translation layer between human intent and machine-readable data — with every UI element mapped to a model input requirement.
